## Objective

This tutorial provides a practical guide for potentiometric surface mapping, focusing on groundwater flow analysis and spatial interpolation techniques. Through step-by-step instructions, you will learn to:

- Extract groundwater data from public databases
- Process well data in GIS software
- Calculate water table elevations
- Apply spatial interpolation methods
- Generate equipotential contours
- Create professional potentiometric maps

The **Catawba River Basin (CRB)** serves as the study area for this practical activity. This basin has well-documented groundwater data available through public databases, making it an ideal location for learning potentiometric mapping techniques.

### Why Catawba River Basin?

- Extensive well inventory available online
- Diverse hydrogeological characteristics
- Good data quality and accessibility
- Representative of typical groundwater mapping scenarios

## Groundwater Data

### Data Sources

Groundwater data is extracted from the **GGIS (Geological and Groundwater Information System)** - an interactive web map interface that provides:

- Well locations and identifiers
- Water level measurements
- Aquifer information (confined/unconfined)
- Well elevation data
- Time-series water depth data
- Data origin and metadata

### Data Components

The downloaded groundwater dataset includes:

- **Well coordinates** (latitude, longitude)
- **Surface elevation** at well locations
- **Static water level depth** (depth from surface to water table)
- **Aquifer type** (confined or unconfined)
- **Temporal data** (time-series measurements)
- **Data metadata** (collection dates, sources, quality)

### Importance of Groundwater Data

Quality groundwater data is essential for:

- Understanding regional flow patterns
- Identifying recharge and discharge areas
- Evaluating contaminant transport
- Planning water resource management
- Assessing aquifer vulnerability
- Modeling hydrogeological conditions
